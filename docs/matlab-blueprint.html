Matlab

Aap bolo. Matlab samjhega.

Indians talk in short voice notes full of nicknames, “parso” and “wahi wala”. Matlab turns each note into the right actions by using what your phone already knows about your life: your people, your calls, your calendar and your habits.

UI system

SahiCar’s design language, applied to messaging

Keep it simple to scan and simple to tap. One typeface, a calm paper background, white cards with hairline borders, black pill buttons for the main action, and colour only where it carries meaning.

Rules we keep from SahiCar

  • One accent, used sparingly. Blue marks what Matlab understood; everything else stays ink and paper.
  • Black pill for the one main action on each card (Send, Do it, Confirm). Outlined pills for the rest.
  • Soft-tint chips carry meaning: people are blue, time is amber, money is green, events are violet, danger is red. Each chip also says what it is in words, so it doesn’t rely on colour alone.
  • Phone first. The phone is the product; on desktop the panel sits beside it, on a phone it stacks below.
  • Show the work. Stage lights with timings and a “why” on every guess build trust, the way SahiCar shows how each car is scored.

Risks and fallbacks

  • The transcription model ignores hints: fix names after transcription instead, by matching how names sound against the contacts. Still measurable.
  • 4–6 seconds per note: fine, because a voice note isn’t a live call. Show the stage timings; use the smaller model for picking contacts.
  • “No outside help”: Prasad records every clip; Telugu clips are synthetic. Ask Sai or Bala before using anyone else’s voice.
